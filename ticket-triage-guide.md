# EHR Support Ticket Triage Guide

How a support ticket gets worked, from the first call to closure.

## 1. Intake: capture the five facts

Every ticket, phone or portal, starts with the same five facts:

1. Who is calling, and their role (nurse, provider, admissions, billing)
2. Where they are (unit or department)
3. Which patient context, by account or encounter only, never by name in the ticket subject
4. What they were doing when it failed (the exact clicks, if possible)
5. What the system did instead (error text, screenshot, or nothing at all)

## 2. Categorize

- Access: login, password, permissions, role setup
- Workflow: orders, MAR or eMAR, charting, care plans
- Data: missing or wrong information on a chart or report
- Interface: labs, pharmacy, or results not crossing over
- System: slowness, freezing, downtime

## 3. Prioritize

- P1: patient care is blocked now (cannot chart meds, cannot admit). Drop everything.
- P2: one user or one workflow blocked, a workaround exists.
- P3: annoyance, cosmetic issue, or how-to question.
- P4: request: new account, new report, new build.

## 4. First-line fixes before escalating

- Confirm the right patient and the right encounter are open
- Check the user's role and permissions
- Refresh, re-login, and try a second workstation
- Check the interface queue for held lab or pharmacy messages
- Search the knowledge base for the exact error text

## 5. Escalate with a complete packet

A good escalation contains: the five intake facts, what was already tried, the exact error, a screenshot with patient identifiers cropped out, and the business impact (who cannot do what).

## 6. Close properly

State the root cause, the fix, and how it was verified with the user. If the same ticket has appeared three times, open a problem record instead of a fourth copy.
